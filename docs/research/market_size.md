## Q1. Elementary enrolment by province (BC K-7; K-6/K-7/K-8 elsewhere), public and independent

### Takeaway
BC had **401,320 K-7 students in 2025/26** (public 341,012 and independent 60,308, so 15.0% independent), out of 715,785 total K-12 headcount. BC K-7 enrolment peaked in 2024/25 at 405,421 and is now falling because kindergarten cohorts are shrinking. Across Canada, the latest comparable StatCan year (2023/24) shows **~3.72M K-8 students (public + private)**: 3.30M K-7 and 2.87M K-6.

### Inferences
- BC's school-based elementary market is about **400K children** in 2025/26: ~341K public plus ~60K independent, plus ~4.5K registered homeschoolers (see Q6). The BC population estimate for ages 5-12 is 414K (2026), which cross-checks well.
- Canada-wide, the elementary-age market is **~3.3M (K-7) to ~3.7M (K-8)** enrolled students, or ~3.47M children aged 5-12 by population. BC is ~11-12% of the national figure. Ontario (~37%), Quebec (~22%) and Alberta (~14%) are the other large blocks.
- For a BC-first launch, Alberta is the fastest-growing next market (5-12 population still rising in 2026). Ontario is the largest by volume. Quebec requires a francophone, Quebec-curriculum product.
- BC's elementary market is **flat to declining** in the near term. 2025/26 kindergarten (43,709) is ~18% smaller than Grade 7 (53,030), and the BC 5-12 population fell ~2% from 2024 to 2026. Growth plans should assume roughly flat BC student counts.
- The independent-school segment in BC (15% of K-7, growing even as public K declines) is a disproportionately high-income parent segment. It may over-index for a paid parent app.
- The BC ELL share (18.5% of elementary) suggests a sizable newcomer-parent segment that may value curriculum explanation for parents. This is an inference and was not tested.
- Grade configurations differ by province (e.g., BC elementary = K-7; many provinces = K-6 or K-8), so the table gives all three cuts. This note on provincial structures is general knowledge and does not come from the sources above.

### Gaps
- StatCan by-grade tables end at 2023/24. 2024/25 provincial data for provinces other than BC was not retrieved; check the provincial ministries (e.g., Ontario's "Enrolment by grade in elementary schools" open-data file).
- StatCan's private-school counts for some provinces may be incomplete or estimated. This was not verified.

## Q2. French Immersion enrolment (BC and Canada): size, trend, waitlists

### Takeaway
French Immersion (FI) is a sizeable, slowly shrinking niche: **BC had 51,660 FI students in 2025/26, 34,300 of them in K-7 (8.5% of BC elementary)**. That is down from a 2019/20 peak of ~54.9K. Canada had **477,105 FI students in 2023/24**, including 320,922 in K-7 and 358,485 in K-8. CPF and districts say demand still exceeds capacity in many BC districts, which allocate kindergarten FI seats by lottery. A teacher shortage is the binding constraint.

- **BC FI elementary (K-7):** 35,011 (2023/24) → 34,599 (2024/25) → **34,300 (2025/26)**. In 2025/26, 33,785 were public and 515 independent. ### Inferences
- FI families are a natural wedge segment for BC: ~34K K-7 FI students, or roughly 20-25K families. Most FI parents do not speak French and plausibly need help supporting homework in French. This is an unverified product hypothesis, but a bilingual (EN/FR) curriculum-aligned app addresses a specific pain point.
- Nationally, ~321K K-7 / ~358K K-8 FI students (2023/24) form a cross-province FI niche. Ontario holds ~58% of it.
- Declining FI counts appear to be supply-constrained (lotteries, teacher shortages) rather than demand-constrained. Families who lose FI lotteries may be a secondary audience for French-learning supplements (inference).

### Gaps
- No national CPF 2023/24 or 2024/25 FI total or quantified waitlist count was found. Waitlists are not standardized across districts.
- No province-wide count of BC FI waitlisted or lottery-rejected applicants was found.

### Inferences
- Dividing the 6-14 population by family counts gives **~1.36-1.73 children aged 6-14 per family** (Canada 1.36 using the upper-bound family count, 1.73 using the lower bound; BC 1.36 and 1.70). A planning ratio of ~1.4-1.5 children per family is reasonable.
- Applying ~1.45 children per family to the July 2026 population aged 5-12 gives ~2.4M Canadian and ~285K BC families with a child aged 5-12. For BC K-7 specifically (401K students), this gives **~275K BC families with an elementary-age child**. These are derived estimates, not published counts.
- One-parent families make up ~25% of families with a 6-14 child in Canada (557,690 / 2,251,090) and ~22% in BC. This matters for willingness to pay and pricing.
- Families with a child aged 0-5 (Canada 1.65M; BC 198K in 2023) are the upcoming pipeline. BC's 0-5 count is falling (201,380 in 2021 → 198,290 in 2023), which is consistent with shrinking BC kindergarten cohorts.

### Gaps
- No 2021 Census table was found that cross-tabulates households by presence of a child aged exactly 5-12 for BC and Canada. Census custom tabulations could provide it.
- T1FF (tax-filer) coverage differs slightly from the Census. Families that also contain adult children (18+) and have a 6-14 child cannot be isolated from the "mixed" category.

## Q4. Mobile OS share in Canada (smartphone vs tablet) and device ownership in families with kids

### Takeaway
Canada is an **iOS-majority market**: StatCounter shows ~65% iOS vs ~35% Android on both smartphones and tablets (Sept 2026). An iOS-first launch is justified, but Android still reaches about one-third of traffic. ChromeOS is only ~4% of desktop web traffic. Recent public data on tablet and Chromebook ownership in Canadian families with kids is thin. The best available figures are dated MTM surveys showing ~69-74% tablet ownership in Anglophone households with children under 12, and MTM Jr. 2025 showing 62% of kids aged 2-6 use tablets.

- US benchmark (not Canada): 80% of US households with children owned a tablet in 2021, vs 57% of households without children. — [US Census Bureau, 2023](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2023/04/tablets-more-common-in-households-with-children.html)

### Inferences
- iOS-first (iPhone + iPad) covers ~65% of Canadian mobile web traffic. Adding Android brings coverage to ~100% of mobile. A web or PWA version would also reach Chromebook households, but ChromeOS is only ~4% of desktop traffic, so it is not a priority channel for a consumer parent app.
- StatCounter measures share of page views, not installed base or unique users. The iOS skew may overstate iOS's share of devices, because iPhone users browse more. It is still directionally consistent with Canada being an iOS-majority market.
- Tablet access among families with elementary-age kids is plausibly **~70-80%**, based on dated Canadian MTM figures (69-74%) and the US 80% benchmark. This range is an estimate and has not been confirmed by current Canadian data.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 Canadian figure was found for household tablet ownership among families with kids, nor any household Chromebook ownership figure. MTM Jr. subscriber data likely has these but is paywalled.
- No BC-specific mobile OS split was found (StatCounter provides national data only on the free tier).
- No public data was found on the share of BC public schools using Chromebooks vs iPads. This could matter for school-home continuity.

## Q5. Spending on supplemental education: tutoring market, spend per family, usage rates, post-pandemic trends

### Takeaway
Recent, rigorous Canadian data on tutoring usage and spend is scarce. The most-cited usage figure, roughly **one-third of Canadian parents having paid for tutoring**, comes from 2007-2015-era surveys. Market-size estimates from commercial research firms conflict widely, from ~USD 0.83B for online tutoring alone (2023) to "$4.7-9.6B" for all private tutoring with unlabelled currencies, so they should be used cautiously. Typical tutoring costs run **~$120-$700/month** (Globe and Mail, pandemic era) or **~$200-$400/month** for weekly sessions (vendor estimate), which sets a high price anchor against a sub-$20/month app. StatCan household-spending data shows BC households spend more on education than the national average ($2,130 vs $1,658 per household in 2023).

### Cited Findings
**Usage and incidence (mostly dated)**
- One-third of Canadian parents "will turn to tutoring"; a former private-school headmaster estimated Canadian tutoring revenue at more than $1B/year (article c. 2017-2018). — [Convivium, "Here Comes the 24/7 Classroom"](https://www.convivium.ca/articles/here-comes-the-24-7-classroom/)
- A University of Toronto/OISE survey found ~35% of Ontario parents had paid for tutoring by 2015, up from ~25% in 2002. This was reported second-hand; the original study was not verified. — [Cognitive Market Research](https://www.cognitivemarketresearch.com/private-tutoring-market-report); [Convivium](https://www.convivium.ca/articles/here-comes-the-24-7-classroom/)
- An academic survey of 501 Canadian parents (Davies & Aurini, likely early-2000s data) found 9% currently hiring tutors and ~17% having ever done so. These are older, lower-bound figures. — [ResearchGate: "School Choice by Default? Understanding the Demand for Private Tutoring in Canada"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/44837040_School_Choice_by_Default_Understanding_the_Demand_for_Private_Tutoring_in_Canada)
- Pandemic-era demand: a Halifax Oxford Learning franchise owner reported demand up 25-30% with the pandemic. — [Globe and Mail, "Parents budget for tutoring to fill in gaps"](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/investing/personal-finance/household-finances/article-parents-budget-for-tutoring-to-fill-in-gaps-in-childrens-education)
- A Fraser Institute/Leger poll (c. 2021-22) found 67% of Canadian parents said their child had fallen behind due to the pandemic and the government's response. — [Fraser Institute](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/article/most-prairie-parents-feel-pandemic-policies-have-hurt-their-childs-education)
- Academic-performance driver: Canada's PISA math score fell from 532 (2003) to 497 (2022). Commentary links this to parents "turning to tutors". — [Troy Media](https://troymedia.com/education/parents-turning-to-tutors-as-education-standards-decline/)
- Ontario 2022 "Catch Up Payments" gave $200-$250 per child for tutoring and related supports. This was a one-time government subsidy and shows policy recognition of the gap. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/9214051/ontario-parents-to-receive-catch-up-payments-for-school-aged-children/amp)

**Price points**
- Tutoring costs range from $120 to $700 per month depending on the program and frequency (pandemic-era reporting). — [Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/investing/personal-finance/household-finances/article-parents-budget-for-tutoring-to-fill-in-gaps-in-childrens-education)
- Vendor estimate (2025-26): budget ~$200-$400/month for weekly sessions. Online rates run ~$15-$25/hr for student tutors and ~$30-$70/hr for certified teachers. Learning centres charge ~3x the tutor wage of about $24/hr. The source sells tutoring, so treat these figures as indicative. — [Connect Education](https://connect-education.com/post/how-much-tutoring-costs-in-canada)

**Market-size estimates (conflicting; commercial research)**
- Technavio forecasts the Canada private tutoring market will grow by **USD 5.36B** from 2025 to 2029 at ~10% CAGR. This is an increment, not the total market size. — [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/canada-private-tutoring-market-analysis); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/private-tutoring-market-in-canada-to-grow-by-usd-5-36-billion-2025-2029-driven-by-emphasis-on-stem-education-ai-impact-on-market-trends---technavio-302360400.html)
- Cognitive Market Research puts Canada private tutoring revenue at $4.696B (2021), $6.823B (2025) and $14.403B (2033), with a 9.79% CAGR. Currency is unlabelled. — [Cognitive Market Research](https://www.cognitivemarketresearch.com/private-tutoring-market-report)
- Grand View Research puts Canada online tutoring services at **USD 829.9M (2023)**, projected to reach USD 2,248.6M by 2030. — [Grand View Research Horizon](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/online-tutoring-services-market/canada)
- IBISWorld covers "Language Instruction in Canada" (an adjacent market, not academic tutoring) at $323.5M revenue in 2025, with 1,084 businesses. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/canada/market-size/language-instruction/1543/)

**Household spending (StatCan Survey of Household Spending, average across all households, including those without children)** — [StatCan 11-10-0222-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1110022201)

| Category, avg $/household | Canada 2019 | Canada 2021 | **Canada 2023** | BC 2019 | BC 2021 | **BC 2023** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Education (total) | 1,691 | 1,797 | **1,658** | 2,357 | 2,491 | **2,130** |
| Tuition, K-12 schools | 207 | 220 | 201 | 313 | 445 | 279 |
| Other educational services | 77 | 52 | 67 | 101 | 80 | 92 |
| Personal interest courses & lessons (2023 only) | – | – | 196 | – | – | 231 |

- In 2023 (other provinces): Ontario Education $2,021, Alberta $1,954, Quebec $993. Other educational services: Ontario $91, Alberta $81, Quebec $30. — [StatCan 11-10-0222-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1110022201)

**Broader parent school spending**
- Capital One Canada (Aug 2026) reports average annual school-related spending of **$9,080 per child in 2026**, up from $8,836 (2025) and $7,709 (2024), an 18% rise since 2024. Upfront items include technology $473, sports/extracurriculars $856 and lunches/snacks $725. Tutoring is not reported as a category. — [Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/news/industry-news/back-to-school-bill-climbs-to-9080-per-child-survey/393386)
- StatCan Survey of Approaches to Educational Planning (2025): 71% of parents/guardians were saving for their children's postsecondary education. This indicates a strong willingness to invest in education. — [Academica Top Ten](https://academica.ca/top-ten/statscan-shares-2025-results-of-survey-of-approaches-to-educational-planning/)

### Inferences
- BC households outspend the national average on education (+28% in 2023), private K-12 tuition (+39%) and "other educational services" (+37%). Together with BC's 15% independent-school share, this supports BC as a reasonable launch market for a paid product.
- "Other educational services" ($67/household Canada, $92 BC, 2023) likely includes some tutoring but is not tutoring-specific. Because it is averaged over all households (most without school-age children), per-family spend among families with children is much higher. The exact multiplier is unknown.
- **Illustrative sizing arithmetic (the founder should substitute their own price and penetration assumptions).** Each 1% of BC K-7 students (~4,000 children) at $120/yr per child yields ~$0.48M ARR. Each 1% of Canada K-8 (~37,000 children) at $120/yr yields ~$4.5M ARR. A theoretical "TAM" of all ~3.72M Canadian K-8 students at $120/yr is ~$447M/yr. For BC K-7 (~401K) it is ~$48M/yr. This is a ceiling, not a forecast.
- If ~one-third of families pay for tutoring (dated figure), the "already paying for supplemental help" segment in BC is roughly 90K families with elementary-age children (applying one-third to ~275K families). This is an inference built on old data.
- The tutoring market-size estimates are inconsistent (different definitions, currencies and methods) and should not be summed or used as a TAM without caveats. The Grand View online-tutoring figure (USD 830M in 2023, roughly C$1.1B at ~1.35-1.40 CAD/USD) is the most narrowly defined. That exchange rate is an assumption and is not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 Leger, Angus Reid or Ipsos survey reporting the share of Canadian parents currently paying for tutoring or learning apps, or average spend per family, was found. This is the largest data gap. A custom survey (e.g., via Angus Reid Forum or Leger Opinion) is recommended.
- No Oxford Learning, Kumon or Sylvan Canada revenue or enrolment data was publicly available.
- The StatCan SHS definitions for "Other educational services" (whether tutoring is included) were not confirmed.
- No BC-specific tutoring usage data was found.

## Q6. Homeschooling and Distributed/Online Learning in BC

### Takeaway
BC has **~4,477 registered homeschoolers (2025/26)**, ~1.8x the pre-pandemic level (2,455 in 2019/20) and stable since 2022/23. The larger "learn-at-home" segment is **online learning (formerly Distributed Learning, DL)**: **~11,750 K-7 students are enrolled in online learning schools as their primary school (2025/26)**, two-thirds of them in independent online schools. Together, that is ~16K BC families running home-based elementary learning, which is a high-intent niche (~4% of BC K-7).

### Cited Findings
- **BC registered homeschoolers (headcount, all grades):** 2,455 (2019/20) → **5,548 (2020/21 peak)** → 4,516 (2021/22) → 4,123 (2022/23) → 4,333 (2023/24) → 4,446 (2024/25) → **4,477 (2025/26)**. The 2025/26 count is registered with public schools 1,709 and independent schools 2,768. — [BC Data Catalogue: Headcount of Homeschooled Children](https://catalogue.data.gov.bc.ca/dataset/d378b0ba-6fa2-4f7f-a08c-97c697979ec3)
- **BC online learning schools (headcount, "All Elementary" = K-7, students whose authority school is an online school):** Provincial Online Learning Schools 8,475 (2019/20) → 15,764 (2020/21 peak) → 12,860 (2021/22) → 11,118 (2022/23) → 10,651 (2023/24) → 10,805 (2024/25) → **11,136 (2025/26)**. In 2025/26 that is public 3,289 and independent 7,847. District Online Learning Schools K-7 add 521 (2023/24), 702 (2024/25) and **614 (2025/26)**. — [BC Data Catalogue: Student Headcount by Grade](https://catalogue.data.gov.bc.ca/dataset/7114187e-305c-4a79-acbb-3294ba0f53c8)
- **BC online learning, all grades (2025/26):** Provincial OL schools 33,217 (public 19,852, independent 13,365) plus District OL schools 3,232. — [BC Data Catalogue](https://catalogue.data.gov.bc.ca/dataset/7114187e-305c-4a79-acbb-3294ba0f53c8)
- **Funding signals (2024/25 operating grants, Sept 2024 count, from Ministry district funding tables via search excerpts):** home-schooling registrations are funded at $250 per student, and online learning at ~$7,200 per FTE. The provincial totals cited were 1,596 homeschoolers (public-registered) and ~8,877 online-learning FTE (public). — [BC Ministry 2024/25 Operating Grant table, e.g. SD39](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/resource-management/k12funding/24-25/24-25-sd-39-march-2024.pdf)
- **Homeschooling by province (StatCan 37-10-0109-01, 2023/24):** Canada 63,150 (vs 40,608 in 2019/20 and 83,988 at the 2020/21 peak). Alberta 24,279, Ontario 13,134, Quebec 7,350, Manitoba 5,109, Saskatchewan 4,587, **BC 4,341**, Nova Scotia 1,773, New Brunswick 1,617. — [StatCan 37-10-0109-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3710010901)
- Definitions: the BC homeschool count covers school-aged children registered as homeschooled with a public, francophone, DL or independent school. Gr 10-12 homeschoolers can also take online courses and may appear in both counts. — [BC Data Catalogue: working with the homeschool extract](https://catalogue.data.gov.bc.ca/dataset/d378b0ba-6fa2-4f7f-a08c-97c697979ec3/resource/8d15c563-6e09-40d1-b090-906e368f6132/download/working-with-the-headcount-of-homeschooled-children-extract-2024-12.pdf); [SCIDES FAQ: DL vs homeschooling](https://scides.com/guardians/17-faq/89-what-is-the-difference-between-distributed-learning-vs-homeschooling)

### Inferences
- BC's home-learning elementary segment is **~11.75K K-7 online-learning students plus a K-7 share of ~4.5K homeschoolers**, so roughly **14-16K children**. Independent online schools are a large share. Many of these schools (e.g., faith-based DL programs) give families per-student learning-resource allowances. That makes them potential B2B2C or reimbursable-purchase channels. This is a hypothesis, and the allowance amounts were not verified in this research.
- BC's registered homeschooling rate is low compared with Alberta (24K homeschoolers). This is likely because BC families who want home education mostly enrol in funded DL/online schools instead. A product aligned to the BC curriculum fits DL families, since DL programs must follow the BC curriculum (inference).
- Provincial online-learning K-7 enrolment has stabilized ~31% above pre-pandemic levels (11,136 in 2025/26 vs 8,475 in 2019/20). Including district online schools, the 2025/26 figure is 11,750, ~39% above the 2019/20 provincial-only figure. Registered homeschooling is ~1.8x its 2019/20 level. Post-pandemic home learning is now a structural segment rather than a temporary one.

### Gaps
- Per-student learning allowances provided by BC independent DL schools (amounts, and whether app subscriptions qualify) were not researched.
- Counts of students cross-enrolled in online courses (non-authority-school enrolments) are not captured in the headcount data.

## Q7. Canadian K-12 consumer edtech app spend and downloads

### Takeaway
Almost no public, Canada-specific K-12 consumer app spend data exists. The best available figure is a Statista model (2022 base) putting **Canada's entire Education app category at ~€144M revenue (2022)**, roughly C$210M by inference, with **~36M downloads** and ~10.7% projected annual growth. That figure covers all ages and language apps like Duolingo, so the K-8 parent-paid slice is much smaller. Sensor Tower and data.ai country-level education data is behind enterprise paywalls.

### Cited Findings
- Statista Market Insights (2022 base-year projections, accessed via a library proxy; the current public page masks values and reports in USD): Canada Education app revenue €144.0M (2022), with 10.65% annual growth projected for 2022-2027. Downloads 36.07M (2022), average revenue per download €3.99. Revenue split: in-app purchases €77.54M, advertising €64.21M, paid apps €2.27M. — [Statista: Education – Canada](https://www.statista.com/outlook/amo/app/education/canada/)
- The current Statista Education-Canada page values are fully masked (subscription required). The page describes "moderate growth" qualitatively. — [Statista: Education – Canada](https://www.statista.com/outlook/amo/app/education/canada/)
- Overall Canadian mobile app market (all categories): ~$3.0B revenue in 2024 (+~$200M YoY), with average per-person app spend ~$95 (2024) vs ~$55 (2019). The underlying data source is not named, so treat as indicative. — [MadeinCA: Mobile app usage statistics Canada](https://madeinca.ca/mobile-app-usage-statistics-canada/)
- Sensor Tower's public kids taxonomy work (Apr 2023) is global and covers ages 2-5. It describes the "Educational" kids category as showing stable downloads and revenue. No Canada breakout. — [Sensor Tower blog](https://sensortower.com/blog/exploring-the-new-kids-and-toddlers-taxonomy)
- data.ai's 2022 report (via DMN) did not place Canada in the top 20 countries for app consumer spending (all categories). — [DMN](https://dmn.ca/?p=70021)

### Inferences
- If Canada's education app category is ~€144M (2022) growing ~10%/yr, it would be ~€190-210M by 2025-26, roughly C$280-310M at ~1.45-1.50 CAD/EUR. Both the extrapolation and the exchange rate are assumptions. K-8 parent-paid subscriptions are plausibly a small fraction of this, since language learning, adult learning and test prep dominate category revenue.
- For a founder's SOM, bottom-up math is more defensible than top-down app-category data: enrolled students × families × price × attainable penetration (see Q1, Q3 and Q5).

### Gaps
- No public Sensor Tower or data.ai figures were found for Canadian K-12 or kids-education app downloads or spend for 2023-2026. The free Statista page is masked.
- No public revenue or subscriber figures were found for competitor apps in Canada (e.g., IXL, Khan Academy Kids (free), Prodigy (Canadian, Oakville ON), Mathletics, SplashLearn). Prodigy's Canadian user counts were not researched in this pass.
