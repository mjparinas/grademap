## 1. BC's redesigned curriculum and K-12 Student Reporting Policy: what changed, and is there evidence of parent confusion or criticism?

### Takeaway
BC replaced K-9 letter grades with a four-point Provincial Proficiency Scale (Emerging, Developing, Proficient, Extending), effective July 1, 2023. It sits on a "Know-Do-Understand" curriculum that many parents never saw in school. The strongest evidence of confusion is a 2024 Leger poll commissioned by the Fraser Institute: most parents could not correctly define the terms, and 64% of BC parents could not identify "Emerging." The Ministry's own parent materials spend much of their space explaining what the terms do *not* mean. That points to a real "decoding" need, although defenders say the scale is no vaguer than letter grades.

### Cited Findings
**Policy mechanics (official)**
- The K-12 Student Reporting Policy took effect July 1, 2023. For K-9, use of the Provincial Proficiency Scale is a provincial requirement, and a district FAQ states that letter grades "cannot be requested or used" for K-9 students — [gov.bc.ca student reporting page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/legislation-policy/public-schools/student-reporting); [SD23 student reporting FAQ (district page, via search excerpt)](https://www.sd23.bc.ca/student-reporting)
- Official parent definitions from the Ministry one-pager:
  - "Emerging means your child is beginning to understand something… may need more support… Emerging doesn't mean your child is unsuccessful."
  - "Developing means your child understands some things… but still has other areas to work on. Like Emerging, it doesn't mean your child is unsuccessful."
  - "Proficient is the goal for your child. It's also the goal for all students."
  - "Extending… is not the goal for all students in every area of learning."
  - [BC Ministry, K-12 Student Reporting: Information for Parents and Caregivers (PDF)](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/K-12-student-reporting-one-pager-for-parents-and-caregivers.pdf)
- The same one-pager specifies five reporting events a year (3 written, 2 informal such as parent-teacher conferences), written feedback in K-12, student self-assessment of Core Competencies, and goal-setting in K-12. Grades 10-12 keep letter grades and percentages "to support their entry into post-secondary." The one-pager frames the change as helping students "focus on learning instead of competing for marks" — [BC Ministry parent one-pager](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/K-12-student-reporting-one-pager-for-parents-and-caregivers.pdf); [BC educator criteria doc](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/criteria-and-considerations-for-written-reports-for-educators.pdf)
- Sources differ on how the five communications are counted. Some districts count 2 written Learning Updates, 2 informal updates and 1 year-end Summary of Learning. The Summary of Learning includes proficiency levels, strengths and growth areas, student self-reflection and goal setting, and attendance — [SD62 regulation](https://www.sd62.bc.ca/sites/default/files/2023-10/B-335%20Regulations%20-%20Communicating%20Student%20Learning%20and%20Reporting.pdf); [Peace River North DPAC deck](https://prn.bc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2022/10/DPAC-K-12-Reporting-Policy-Framework.pdf)
- The curriculum is built on a "Know-Do-Understand" model:
  - Content (Know)
  - Curricular Competencies (Do: skills, strategies, processes)
  - Big Ideas (Understand: generalizations meant to endure beyond a grade)
  - [curriculum.gov.bc.ca, Introduction to BC's curriculum redesign](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/rethinking-curriculum)
- The three Core Competencies are Communication, Thinking, and Personal and Social. Curricular Competencies in each subject are built on them — [curriculum.gov.bc.ca competencies](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/competencies); [BC curriculum glossary](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/supports/glossary.pdf)
- A UBC learning-design resource says the Ministry's suggested Big Ideas are "not 'mandated'" and "not formally assessed," and that teachers may write or co-develop their own. This is a teacher resource, not Ministry policy — [UBC Learning Design blog](https://blogs.ubc.ca/learningdesign/?p=1014)
- The Ministry says the redesigned curriculum has been in place in all grades since 2019 and still provides "a strong foundation in reading, writing, and math" — [BC Ministry parent one-pager](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/K-12-student-reporting-one-pager-for-parents-and-caregivers.pdf)

**Evidence of parent confusion and criticism**
- Fraser Institute / Leger poll (reported October 24, 2024), Canadian parents:
  - 98% say regular, clear assessment is important.
  - ~93% found the letter grade "A" clear.
  - 57% found "Emerging" unclear and 28% could identify its meaning.
  - 58% found "Extending" unclear.
  - About half said letter grades would be easier to understand; the rest don't mind descriptive grading.
  - [Global News, Oct 24, 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10828759/bc-parents-letter-grades-descriptive-grading-schools/)
- Search excerpts of the same Fraser material report that 68% could correctly identify what "A" means, 26% could identify "Extending" (Global News says 28%, a minor conflict), and **64% of B.C. parents were unable to correctly identify "emerging"** from a list. The report states: "Even in B.C., where descriptive grades… have already been rolled out, parents struggle to correctly match the report card terms with their definitions" — [Fraser Institute commentary (403 on fetch; via search excerpt)](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/bc-parents-confused-by-governments-new-descriptive-grading)
- A separate Fraser piece says fewer than one-third of parents could explain the meaning of emerging, developing, proficient and extending. Critics argue that "developing" and "emerging" mean similar things in everyday English — [Fraser Institute, "B.C. parents and students deserve report cards they can understand" (via search excerpt)](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/bc-parents-and-students-deserve-report-cards-they-can-understand)
- **Bias caveat:** the Fraser Institute is a free-market think tank that opposed the change and commissioned the poll — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10828759/bc-parents-letter-grades-descriptive-grading-schools/)
- Counterpoint: an educator quoted by The Tyee says the proficiency scale is no more vague or confusing than letter grades or percentages — [The Tyee, Sept 1, 2023](https://thetyee.ca/News/2023/09/01/BC-End-Letter-Grades-Report-Cards/)
- Counterpoint: in Richmond's pilot, families reported that the scale gave clear feedback and that digital portfolios helped them see learning develop over the year. This is a district survey, not independent — [SD38 Richmond (via search excerpt)](https://sd38.bc.ca/node/372)
- A Globe and Mail opinion piece argues report cards "should be clear, not confusing" (headline only reviewed) — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-school-report-cards-should-be-clear-not-confusing/)
- Districts are producing their own explainer analogies. Peace River North, for example, explains the scale with a driving comparison — [SD60 Peace River North](https://www.prn.bc.ca/report-card-renovations-proficiency-scale-a-driving-comparison/)
- Commercial tutoring companies are publishing "report card decoded" content aimed at BC parents. One says parents "frequently translate 'Developing' as a C or a 'barely passing'" and advises them to "resist the urge to convert everything to A/B/C." This is a market signal, not neutral evidence — [Tutriva blog](https://tutriva.com/blog/bc-proficiency-scale-report-card-decoded/); [Connect Education guide](https://www.connect-education.com/post/navigating-british-columbias-new-report-cards-a-parents-guide)
- Self-assessment of Core Competencies is part of written reports. Vancouver School Board school pages say reports combine written comments, a proficiency scale and student self-assessment of Core Competencies (search excerpt; exact page uncertain) — [VSB school assessment & reporting page](https://vsb.bc.ca/queen-alexandra/assessment-and-reporting)

### Inferences
- The Ministry's official definitions are mostly *reassurance* ("doesn't mean unsuccessful") rather than *actionable* guidance. They do not say what skill a child is missing or what to practise. The policy (written feedback, Summary of Learning, self-assessment) exists, but parents still face the step from "Developing in Numeracy" to "what do we do tonight." That gap is the product opportunity.
- Report-card decoding is already a content-marketing tactic for BC tutoring companies. Demand is real, but a static explainer page is not defensible. An interactive, child-specific decoder (scan or enter the report card, see the learning standards for that grade, get practice) would be.
- The disagreement is about *understanding*, not about whether parents want feedback (98% want clear assessment). A product can stay neutral in the letter-grades debate and still serve both camps.

### Gaps
- No BCCPAC (BC Confederation of Parent Advisory Councils) statement or survey on the reporting policy was found. The search returned only an archived 2003 BCCPAC resolution and unrelated "BCCPA" (care providers) results.
- No Reddit threads (r/britishcolumbia, r/vancouver, r/BCTeachers) were retrieved. Search tools did not surface them, so this note contains no first-person parent quotes.
- No petitions to restore letter grades in BC were found.
- No CBC or Vancouver Sun article on parent confusion was fetched in full. A 2023 Global News "parent/student react" piece exists ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/9792360/parent-student-react-no-letter-grades)) but was not read.
- No Ministry-run or independent post-2024 evaluation of parent understanding was found.

## 2. Foundational skills concerns: BC FSA, PISA/PCAP, literacy screening and the science-of-reading movement

### Takeaway
There is credible evidence that foundational math and reading are slipping in Canada and BC:
- PISA math fell 35 points between 2003 and 2022.
- In BC, roughly 40% of Grade 7 students have not met FSA numeracy expectations since 2021-22.
- BC's provincial Grade 4 literacy average "dropped considerably" in 2024-25.

Policy is moving toward structured literacy and early screening: Ontario Right to Read (2022), BC mandatory K screening from 2025-26, and Alberta screening. This "back to basics / science of reading" current gives parents a reason to want targeted foundational practice and diagnostics.

### Cited Findings
**BC FSA (Grades 4 and 7)**
- The FSA tests Grade 4 and Grade 7 literacy and numeracy each fall (October to November). Results are not part of report cards — [BC Ministry FSA page](https://www.gov.bc.ca/foundation-skills-assessment); [SD83 FSA information](https://www.sd83.bc.ca/fsa-information)
- The FSA reports three levels (Emerging, On Track, Extending), with "Developing" and "Proficient" combined into "On Track." That is a different scale from report cards, which adds another layer of terminology for parents — [SD83 FSA information (via search excerpt)](https://www.sd83.bc.ca/fsa-information)
- Grade 4 numeracy: from 2016-17 to 2023-24, "nearly two-thirds" met expectations and one-quarter to one-third did not — [The Tyee, Oct 7, 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)
- Grade 7 numeracy: before the pandemic just over half met expectations. **From 2021-22 onward, roughly 40% did not meet expectations** — [The Tyee, Oct 7, 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)
- Richmond's Sept 2025 district report:
  - FSA literacy scores have been declining in the district and provincially.
  - "The provincial average dropped considerably this year for Grade 4 literacy."
  - 15% of Grade 4 students did not write the FSA in 2024-25.
  - The move of the test from spring to fall of Grade 4 may affect trends.
  - [Richmond SD38 Enhanced Student Learning Report 2025](https://sd38.bc.ca/app/uploads/2026/02/SD38-ESLR-Report-2025.pdf)
- 2021-22 FSA data is a new series because reading and writing were combined into literacy and a new vendor ran the test, so long-run comparisons are limited — [Richmond SD38 report / search excerpt](https://sd38.bc.ca/app/uploads/2026/02/SD38-ESLR-Report-2025.pdf)
- Abbotsford's 2024-25 report shows literacy and numeracy proficiency largely fell in Grades 4 and 7. Grade 7 Indigenous literacy dropped 14 points to 49% proficient. This was reported via a tutoring company blog, so verify against the district report — [Best Brains Abbotsford blog](https://bestbrains.com/ca-en/abbotsford/article/what-does-bc-fsa-test-actually-measure)
- Provincial open data covering 2021/22 to 2025/26 is available as a CSV. It was not analysed here — [Open Government Portal, BC FSA dataset](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/5554165d-e365-422f-bf85-4f6e4c9167dc)

**Explanations offered (from The Tyee, Oct 2025)**
- Melanie Raymond, school psychologist:
  - Numeracy problems stem from "a lack of practice."
  - Many students were not taught to decode words.
  - An estimated 20-25% struggle with reading, against 3-5% who would struggle even with good instruction.
- Anna Stokke, math professor: BC's curriculum under-emphasizes memorizing math facts. BC Grade 5 expects recall of multiplication facts but "memorization is not required," while most jurisdictions expect automaticity by Grade 3 or 4. She supports programs like JUMP Math.
- Cynthia Nicol (UBC): sees no need for an overhaul.
- A primary teacher cites increased solo screen use and less conversation at home.
- BC Teachers' Council surveys (2015, 2021) found teacher education programs did not prepare new teachers to teach reading, writing or math.
- Minister Lisa Beare: "Without those solid foundational skills, we end up with kids that have gaps."
- [The Tyee, Oct 7, 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)

**PISA 2022 and PCAP 2023**
- Canada's PISA 2022 math mean was 497 against an OECD average of 472. CMEC notes average math scores "are declining over time" — [CMEC PISA 2022 infographic](https://www.cmec.ca/docs/pisa2022/PISA-2022-INFORGRAPHICS-EN.pdf)
- Canada's math score fell 35 points from 2003 to 2022 — [Globe and Mail (via search excerpt)](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-math-scores-of-canadian-students-are-declining-raising-concern-about) — and 15 points from 2018 to 2022 — [Radio-Canada International](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/rci/en/news/2032651/math-and-reading-scores-for-canadian-students-declined-sharply-since-2018-study-finds)
- The share of students below baseline proficiency (Level 2) in math rose 7 percentage points versus 2012 — [OECD PISA 2022 Canada country note](https://oecd.org/publication/pisa-2022-results/country-notes/canada-901942bb)
- C.D. Howe:
  - Population-weighted math declines from the benchmark year to 2022 were -34 points in the four large provinces and -50 in the six smaller ones.
  - Newfoundland and Labrador is the first province below the OECD math average.
  - Quebec is the only province without a math decline, which C.D. Howe credits to more rigorous teacher training.
  - [C.D. Howe, "Case of the Boiling Frogs"](https://cdhowe.org/?p=125438); [C.D. Howe, "Time to Address Canada's Falling Math Scores"](https://cdhowe.org/publication/time-to-address-canadas-falling-math-scores/)
- PCAP 2023 (Grade 8): about 31,500 students. Quebec had the top math average; Alberta scored 513 against a Canadian mean of 510. Science was the major domain. BC's 2023 math figure was not retrieved — [CMEC PCAP 2023 Highlights](https://www.cmec.ca/docs/pcap/pcap2023/PCAP2023_Highlights_FINAL_EN.pdf); [Alberta release, June 2026](https://kutd.awna.com/releases/alberta-students-rank-top-in-canada/)
- For context, in PCAP 2019, 88% of BC students met the expected math level — [BC PCAP 2019 report](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/assessment/pcap2019-website-report.pdf)

**Literacy screening and the science-of-reading movement**
- BC (September 2025 release, read via search excerpt):
  - Kindergarten students are screened for early reading starting in 2025-26.
  - Districts choose tools meeting Ministry criteria.
  - The Ministry plans a standardized K-3 approach, including a single made-in-BC screening tool.
  - [BC Gov News 2025ECC0025-000851](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025ECC0025-000851)
- Mission SD frames the mandate as K-3 screening with "evidence-based, standardized screeners." It plans Acadience Reading for Grades 1-3 in 2026-27 (one district's plan) — [SD75 Mission literacy assessments](https://sd75curriculum.com/literacy/literacy-assessments/)
- BCTF's Carole Gordon says mandatory screening is useful only with more support staff — [The Tyee, Oct 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)
- The Ministry released "B.C. Learning Pathways" resources and is setting up a K-12 Literacy Supports Community of Practice — [The Tyee, Oct 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)
- The Fraser Institute argues screening alone won't improve reading; instruction must shift to structured literacy and phonics — [Fraser Institute commentary](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/screening-tools-alone-wont-improve-reading-skills-bc-students)
- Ontario Right to Read (OHRC, Feb 28, 2022):
  - 157 recommendations.
  - Calls for structured literacy / science of reading in the K and Grades 1-8 Language curriculum.
  - Calls for twice-yearly standardized screening for K-2.
  - Calls for evidence-based interventions.
  - [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Read_inquiry_report); [OHRC report, Early screening chapter](https://www3.ohrc.on.ca/en/right-read-inquiry-report/9-early-screening)
- OHRC: timed oral reading fluency measures are "excellent predictors of future reading from about the middle of Grade 1 onward" — [OHRC Right to Read ch. 9](https://www3.ohrc.on.ca/en/right-read-inquiry-report/9-early-screening)
- Ontario PPM 168 requires screening in Year 2 of kindergarten, Grade 1 and Grade 2 with Ministry-approved tools — [Ontario PPM 168](https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-ontario-policy-and-program-direction/policyprogram-memorandum-168)
- Ontario's revised language curriculum brought back phonics and cursive, with at least 30 minutes of protected daily reading. It applied to Grade 1+ from 2023, and kindergarten was planned for Sept 2025 — [CBC, Sept 2024](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7313187); [CTV](https://toronto.ctvnews.ca/ontario-to-revamp-approach-to-literacy-in-schools-1.5799524)
- The movement is spreading: Saskatchewan has joined calls to treat reading disabilities as a human rights issue (headline) — [MSVU](https://www.msvu.ca/reading-disabilities-are-a-human-rights-issue-saskatchewan-joins-calls-to-address-barriers/). Alberta runs provincial literacy and numeracy screening with an approved screener list — [Alberta screening page](https://www.alberta.ca/literacy-and-numeracy-screening); [Alberta approved screeners PDF](https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/ecc-literacy-numeracy-gib-screeners-approved-list.pdf)
- Ontario EQAO 2024-25 (released Dec 3, 2025):
  - Grade 3 math: 64% met the provincial standard (61% in 2023-24, 60% in 2022-23).
  - Grade 6 math: 51% (50% in each of the two prior years).
  - [OCDSB achievement data](https://www.ocdsb.ca/about-us/facts-figures/achievement-data); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11557546/ford-government-eqao-results-review)
- Minister Calandra said "back to basics" progress is too slow and launched an expert review. Search excerpts report that 42% of Grade 9 students did not meet the math standard (verify) — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11557546/ford-government-eqao-results-review)

### Inferences
- Parents now see "foundational gaps" coverage in mainstream outlets (Tyee, Globe, Global, CBC), along with screening rollouts that will produce new data on their child (BC K screening, Ontario K-2). That creates demand for help interpreting screening results and doing targeted, phonics-aligned home practice.
- Expert commentary keeps returning to math-fact fluency (Stokke) and "lack of practice" (Raymond). That supports short, daily, spaced-retrieval practice as the core loop of the app, as long as it doesn't copy IXL's punitive scoring (see Q5).
- Three different scales appear in BC: the 4-level report card, the 3-level FSA "On Track", and letter grades in Grades 10-12. That supports one consistent "where is my child against grade-level standards" view.

### Gaps
- Exact provincial BC FSA percentages for 2024-25 and 2025-26 were not retrieved. They are in the open-data CSV and could be downloaded and analysed.
- BC's PISA 2022 and PCAP 2023 math scores were not retrieved.
- It is unclear whether BC has released its single made-in-BC K-3 screening tool, or whether screening results go to parents.
- No evidence was found on how parents respond to screening results, for example whether they seek outside help.

## 3. French Immersion parents who don't speak French: pain points and existing supports

### Takeaway
French Immersion (FI) is popular in BC: over 53,000 students, nearly 10% of enrolment, in older CBC data. It is supply-constrained by teacher shortages. Schools tell families that parents "will not need to speak French," and most support is generic (encouragement, French media, board homework sites). Little dedicated tooling exists to help non-francophone parents understand *what* their child is learning in French. Evidence that this specific gap drives FI attrition is thin.

### Cited Findings
- BC FI enrolment hit a record of over 53,000 students, close to 10% of students. Canadian Parents for French (CPF) says demand would be higher if districts could meet it — [CBC BC (circa 2019)](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/french-immersion-bc-1.4987546)
- CPF BC & Yukon found that 86% of BC districts said it is "challenging" or "very challenging" to fill FI teaching positions, and that BC produces about one-fifth of the FI teachers it needs (report date not shown) — [BCATML summary of CPF report](https://bcatml.org/news/canadian-parents-for-french-bc-yukon-release-report)
- The shortage has led to an Abbotsford FI school using an instructor who couldn't speak French (2018), kindergarten entry caps (Sooke), and about 1,000 students turned away per year across 23 communities (older CBC) — [Global News 2018](https://globalnews.ca/news/4613389/teacher-shortage-leads-abbotsford-french-immersion-school-to-use-instructor-who-cant-speak-the-language); [CBC Salmon Arm](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/french-immersion-shortage-sparks-parent-camp-out-in-salmon-arm-1.2593521)
- The standard school message is reassurance. Winnipeg SD: "French Immersion is designed for families who do not speak French at home. Parents will not need to speak French to assist their child." Report cards and family communications are in English — [Winnipeg SD, Victoria-Albert FI page](https://www.winnipegsd.ca/victoria-albert/french-immersion)
- Rainbow DSB (Ontario) advice for parents is encouragement, French radio in the background, or taking a French course. This is a 2016 document — [Rainbow DSB FAQ](https://lasalleelementary.rainbowschools.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/63/2016/08/FAQs.pdf)
- A Sudbury-area board built a French homework website after parents repeatedly asked how to help without speaking French. It includes video clips on supporting reading, writing and math at home (older) — [CBC Sudbury](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/sudbury/sudbury-school-board-launches-french-homework-website-1.1023770)
- A school flyer points FI parents to fslhomeworktoolbox.ca (not verified) — [Toronto Catholic school FI flyer](https://olgraceau.archtoronto.org/siteassets/media/pdfs/sja-fi-flyer.pdf)
- A tutoring company advises having children teach parents vocabulary or translate labels (commercial source) — [Oxford Learning](https://oxfordlearning.com/how-do-i-help-my-child-in-french-when-i-dont-speak-it/)
- A Simon Fraser University thesis interviewing Richmond, BC parents found "a lack of program information being received by parents," leaving them "unprepared and uncertain" about whether FI fits their child — [SFU thesis (2023)](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2023-09/etd22578.pdf)
- Attrition: only 33% of Alberta students complete FI to Grade 12 — [Medicine Hat CBE](https://mhcbe.ab.ca/our-division/news/post/improving-french-immersion). Older research lists academic difficulties, teaching quality, "parent's influence," limited course choice, peer pressure and workload as reasons for leaving, without isolating parents' inability to help — [Obadia & Thériault 1995 (via UBC blog)](https://blogs.ubc.ca/gloweblog/files/2018/04/LOWE-Obadia-and-Theriault-1995-Attrition-in-French-immersion-Programs.docx)
- Free French-language resources exist: TFO and Radio-Canada kids sites, and Quebec's Alloprof (free, French-first, Quebec curriculum) — [search summary of board resources](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/sudbury/sudbury-school-board-launches-french-homework-website-1.1023770); [Alloprof annual report 2021-22](https://cms.alloprof.qc.ca/sites/default/files/2022-11/Annual%20report%2021-22%20-%20Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pain point is less "my child can't do the work" and more "I can't see or understand what my child is doing, so I can't tell whether they're behind." Schools' reassurance ("you don't need French") leaves parents without a way to monitor progress. A bilingual product could close this by showing each French activity alongside an English parent view (what the skill is, what the child got right, a suggested English-language conversation prompt).
- FI teacher shortages mean some FI classes may be taught by less-qualified staff. Parents who worry about this are a high-intent segment for supplementary French literacy (phonics in French is a distinct skill set).
- Alloprof shows that high-quality French content can be free (Quebec-funded). An FI product should not compete on French content volume. It should compete on the *parent bridge* (EN parent / FR child) and on alignment with BC or Ontario FI curricula, which differ from Quebec's French-first program.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 BC FI enrolment figures or CPF report were found. The 53,000 figure is dated (~2019).
- No study was found that directly measures non-francophone parents' inability to help as a cause of FI attrition.
- It is unverified whether Alloprof content is usable or marketed outside Quebec, and whether IXL or Khan offer FI-aligned French content for Canadian provinces.
- The BC FI curriculum (Français langue seconde – immersion) was not examined.

## 4. Provincial curriculum differences relevant to expansion: how hard is localization?

### Takeaway
Localization is moderate for math and harder for reporting language, literacy approach and French:
- **Math:** Western and Atlantic provinces share the WNCP math framework lineage (Manitoba, Nova Scotia and PEI explicitly). Ontario and Quebec use distinct curricula, and Alberta is mid-renewal.
- **Reporting and literacy:** each province uses different reporting vocabulary (BC 4-level proficiency scale; Ontario Levels 1-4 and letter grades with EQAO; Quebec percentages with 20/20/60 term weights; Alberta's own scheme). Curricula are also being actively revised (Alberta K-6 2022-2026, Ontario language 2023+, Ontario curriculum pause and review 2025-26).
- A content core of skills mapped per province, plus a province-specific "reporting and expectations" layer, appears feasible.

### Cited Findings
**Ontario**
- The 2020 Grades 1-8 math curriculum was the first new elementary math curriculum in 15 years. It has six strands:
  - A. Social-Emotional Learning Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes
  - B. Number
  - C. Algebra (including coding from Grade 1)
  - D. Data
  - E. Spatial Sense
  - F. Financial Literacy (mandatory Grades 1-8)
  - [Ontario.ca, new math curriculum](https://www.ontario.ca/page/new-math-curriculum-grades-1-8); [Ontario curriculum strands](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/elementary-mathematics/context/the-strands-in-the-mathematics-curriculum); [Global News 2020](https://globalnews.ca/news/7097501/ontario-new-elementary-math-curriculum)

- Khan Academy aligned math content to Ontario and BC Grades 4-6 standards in 2015 via The Learning Partnership — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/the-learning-partnership-helps-bring-khan-academy/)

- Khan Academy Kids is aligned to Common Core and Head Start, not Canadian curricula — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=46)
- IXL is used in 150+ countries "with provincial curricula available" (undated) — [Teach Magazine](https://teachmag.com/?p=4810)

### Inferences
- **Math localization difficulty: low to moderate.**
  - One skill graph mapped to roughly four outcome sets covers most of English Canada: BC, Ontario 2020, Alberta 2022 and WNCP-derived (Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Atlantic).
  - Main deltas are grade placement and province-specific strands: Ontario's coding, financial literacy and SEL, and BC's Big Ideas and Curricular Competencies framing.
- **Literacy localization: moderate.** Provinces are converging on structured literacy (Ontario 2023, Alberta K-6, BC screening), which favours a phonics-based scope and sequence usable across provinces.
- **Reporting localization: high value, low content cost.** Each province needs its own "decoder":
  - BC: E/D/P/E plus FSA On Track.
  - Ontario: Levels 1-4 and letter grades, plus EQAO.
  - Quebec: percentages and term weighting.
  - Alberta: varies.
  This is mostly copy and mapping work, not new practice content, which makes it a cheap differentiator against IXL and Khan.
- **Curriculum churn creates a recurring parent need.** Alberta 2022-2027, Ontario's pause and review, and BC's K-3 screening all leave parents unfamiliar with what their child is learning. "What's changing this year in my province" content is evergreen.
- **Quebec is a poor early target.** Alloprof is free, government-supported, French-first and already does parent report-card explainers. Quebec also had the best math results (PISA, PCAP).
- **BC-first is a reasonable beachhead.** BC has a distinctive, polarizing reporting system and declining FSA results, and its incumbents (IXL, Khan) default to US-order content.

### Gaps
- Saskatchewan, Manitoba and Atlantic elementary report card formats were not researched.
- Alberta's elementary reporting scale was not confirmed.
- No line-by-line outcome comparison (BC vs. Ontario vs. Alberta math by grade) was done.
- Ontario's post-pause Grades 1-8 math plans for 2026-27 were not confirmed.
- IXL's current Canadian provincial alignment depth was not verified.

## 5. What parents want from learning apps, and common complaints about IXL and Khan Academy

### Takeaway
IXL's dominant complaint is its punitive SmartScore, which parents link to stress, tears and avoidance, followed by cost and cancellation friction. Khan Academy's complaints are a confusing layout, insufficient explanation and needing adult guidance. Canadian parents broadly accept educational screen use but worry about volume. Research says the most effective parent role is academic socialization and low-friction coaching, not hands-on homework help. Direct Canadian survey data on learning-app preferences is scarce.

### Cited Findings
**IXL complaints**
- Common Sense Media has 1,650 parent reviews (6,570 kid reviews). The AI summary describes a "punishing scoring system, which frequently penalizes them harshly for small mistakes," distress described as "torturous," repetitiveness, and parents wanting children to "avoid the platform at all costs" — [Common Sense Media, IXL parent reviews](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/website-reviews/ixl/user-reviews/adult)
- Search excerpt from the same page (~1,638 reviews at the time) mentions "stress and demotivation, leading to tears and feelings of inadequacy" — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/website-reviews/ixl/user-reviews/adult)
- Trustpilot reviewers say a wrong answer costs "8-20 points," that typos and misclicks are penalized, and that the site is "mentally exhausting… especially [for] those with ADHD or Autism." One reports being unable to find how to stop paying — [Trustpilot IXL reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ixl.com?page=10); [UK Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/ixl.com?page=8)
- A competitor blog says one wrong answer in the 70-99 SmartScore range can erase 7-20 points. Competitor-authored, so treat with caution — [Nibble IXL review](https://nibble-app.com/blog/ixl-review)
- App-store ratings are higher (~4/5 iOS, ~4.3 Android, per a competitor blog), so sentiment depends on the venue — [Monster Math blog](https://www.monstermath.app/blog/ixl-reviews-from-real-users-should-you-use-it-in-2026)
- Price:
  - US family pricing starts at $9.95/month or $79/year for one subject, and $19.95/month or $159/year for core subjects (June 2026) — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-ixl-cost/)
  - Additional children are about $4/month or $40/year — [Nibble IXL cost](https://nibble-app.com/blog/ixl-cost)
  - Older CAD figures show $12.95/month for math and $19.95/month for math plus English — [UBC IXL analyst blog](https://blogs.ubc.ca/ixlanalystreport/face-3-the-buyer/)
  - There is no free tier; there is a 30-day money-back guarantee — [search summary of IXL family pages](https://ie.ixl.com/membership/family)

**Khan Academy complaints**
- Common Sense Media (106 parent reviews, ages 9+) praises it as a free resource for independent learning. Complaints are a "confusing layout, lack of adequate explanations," content that is overwhelming for younger students, and frequent need for parent guidance (AI summary) — [Common Sense Media, Khan Academy parent reviews](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/website-reviews/khan-academy/user-reviews/adult)
- Khan Academy Kids (ages 2-8; classroom tools mainly Pre-K to Gr 2) gets mostly positive reviews (only 19 on CSM), with some technical glitches — [Common Sense Media, Khan Kids](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/khan-academy-kids/user-reviews/adult); [Educators Technology review, Sept 2026](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2026/09/khan-academy-kids-review.html)
- Khan content follows US sequencing (see Q4) — [Khan Academy community](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/fr/community/posts/360074004591-Canadian-Curriculum)

**Screen time and Canadian parent attitudes**
- Angus Reid (likely ~2019): nearly 9 in 10 parents of kids aged 2-12 say their children use devices 60+ minutes a day. 46% worry about too much screen time, and 89% see devices as a potentially useful teaching aid — [Angus Reid Institute](https://angusreid.org/screen-time-kids/)
- A recent Canadian review citing a national survey (parent n=385) found over 80% of parents see limiting screen use as key to healthy development, and nearly two-thirds worry about their child's screen time — [PMC review, Screen Use and Child and Adolescent Health in Canada](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12705222/)
- Liaison Strategies (2026): only 1 in 20 households with children would remove laptops and tablets entirely from elementary schoolwork, compared with 22% of households without children — [Liaison Strategies](https://press.liaisonstrategies.ca/parents-more-open-to-classroom-screen-use-than-canadians-without-kids/)
- The Canadian Paediatric Society publishes guidance on healthy screen use for school-aged children — [CPS position statement](https://cps.ca/en/documents/position/digital-media)
- CBC reported an observational study linking more screen time to lower test scores (correlational) — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/screentime-test-scores-9.6935108)

**Research on effective parent involvement**
- Hill & Tyson (2009), a meta-analysis of 50 studies and 50,000+ students (middle school):
  - **Academic socialization** (communicating expectations, linking schoolwork to goals) had the strongest positive link to achievement.
  - Homework help was inconsistent and sometimes *negative*, possibly because of pressure, methods differing from teachers', or reverse causation.
  - [Harvard GSE](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/09/10/parental-involvement-puzzle); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/05/090519134711.htm)
- Maloney et al. (2015), Grades 1-2: children of math-anxious parents learned less math and grew more math-anxious, but only when those parents helped frequently with homework. Maloney's group recommends tools that reduce parent anxiety and scaffold how to help. A larger replication (3,018 K-3 students) found negative main effects but not the interaction — [Maloney et al. 2015 (PDF)](https://sites.temple.edu/cognitionlearning/files/2015/09/Maloney-et-al-2015.pdf); [UChicago Science of Learning](https://voices.uchicago.edu/scienceoflearning/news-archive/news/parents-math-anxiety-can-rub-kids)
- Bedtime Math app RCT (Chicago): families reading math stories together raised first graders' math achievement, and children of math-anxious parents caught up. Usage fell to under once a week by year two — [KQED MindShift / NPR](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/52749/how-to-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math)
- READY4K! text-message RCT (San Francisco, ~1,031 families): three texts a week (fact, tip, growth) raised parent involvement by 0.15-0.29 SD and early literacy by ~0.11 SD, with larger gains for lower-skilled children (+0.31). Rated "Strong" by Evidence for ESSA. The lead author is affiliated with the commercial program — [NBER w20659](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20659.pdf); [Stanford CEPA working paper](https://cepa.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/One%20Step%20CEPA%20Working%20Paper%206%201%2017.pdf); [Evidence for ESSA](https://evidenceforessa.org/?p=1852)
- A 2025 *Child Development* article is titled "Learning apps at home prepare children for school" (title only reviewed) — [Niklas et al. 2025, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cdev.14184)

**Spending context**
- A 2025 *Canadian Journal of Political Science* paper uses StatCan's Survey of Household Spending to study how income and parental education relate to tutoring and lessons spending — [Cambridge CJPS](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-political-science-revue-canadienne-de-science-politique/article/income-inequality-and-parent-education-spending-in-canada/511CC3F710DBC22425C6D7DA56A2115F)

### Inferences
- **The anti-IXL wedge is emotional safety plus usability:**
  - Mastery tracking that never takes points away for a single error (e.g., streaks of correct answers, or mastery estimated over time).
  - Forgiveness for typos and misclicks.
  - Explanations shown after errors.
  - Clear pricing and one-tap cancel.
  This answers the most frequent and most emotional complaint directly.
- **The anti-Khan wedge is guidance plus local fit.** Khan is free but "requires guidance" and follows US order. A product that tells the parent exactly what to do this week (for example 10 minutes on skill X, aligned to the BC Grade 3 standard) covers what Khan leaves out.
- **The parent should be a coach, not a homework tutor.** Hill & Tyson and Maloney both suggest parent homework help can backfire. Bite-sized parent prompts (READY4K-style texts, Bedtime-Math-style conversation starters) have RCT support. That argues for a parent channel (weekly digest, push or SMS tips, conversation scripts) rather than asking parents to sit and teach.
- **Screen time.** Parents accept educational devices but worry about volume, so short sessions (10-15 minutes) with a visible cap, plus off-screen activities, address both concerns.

### Gaps
- No Canadian survey was found on what parents specifically want from learning apps (features, willingness to pay, short daily practice). This is a key gap and may need primary research (parent interviews or a survey).
- No Reddit or parenting-forum threads on IXL or Khan from Canadian parents were retrieved.
- IXL's current CAD price was not verified on ca.ixl.com.
- Khan Academy's French offering and Canadian-specific features were not verified.
- The Common Sense Media aggregate star ratings for IXL and Khan were not captured.

## 6. Differentiation ideas and the evidence behind them

### Takeaway
The best-supported differentiators are:
1. A province-specific **report card and screening decoder** that turns labels into next steps.
2. **"What's my kid learning this term"** by grade and province, given curriculum churn and opaque competency language.
3. **Parent coaching scripts and micro-nudges**, which have RCT evidence.
4. **Non-punitive, short daily practice** focused on foundational skills (math facts, phonics), as the direct counter to IXL's SmartScore.
5. **A bilingual EN-parent / FR-child bridge** for French Immersion families.

Indigenous perspectives alignment and teacher sharing are plausible in BC but need partnership and careful handling. The evidence here supports them as table stakes in BC rather than as headline differentiators.

### Cited Findings (evidence for each idea)
- **Report card decoder.**
  - Most Canadian parents could not define Emerging (28%) or Extending (~26-28%), and 64% of BC parents could not identify Emerging — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10828759/bc-parents-letter-grades-descriptive-grading-schools/); [Fraser Institute](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/bc-parents-confused-by-governments-new-descriptive-grading)
  - Official Ministry definitions are reassurance-oriented — [BC Ministry one-pager](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/K-12-student-reporting-one-pager-for-parents-and-caregivers.pdf)
  - Tutoring firms and Alloprof already publish static decoders — [Tutriva](https://tutriva.com/blog/bc-proficiency-scale-report-card-decoded/); [Alloprof](https://www.alloprof.qc.ca/en/parents/articles/report-cards-and-school-system/understanding-your-childs-report-card-k1472)
- **"What's my kid learning this term."**
  - BC's Know-Do-Understand structure and Big Ideas are teacher-flexible and not formally assessed — [curriculum.gov.bc.ca](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/rethinking-curriculum); [UBC Learning Design](https://blogs.ubc.ca/learningdesign/?p=1014)
  - Alberta's K-6 rollout through 2026 and Ontario's 2025 pause and 2026 kindergarten changes keep shifting what children learn — [Alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/curriculum); [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/ontario-announces-pause-on-school-curriculum-changes-including-kindergarten-overhaul/)
- **Parent coaching scripts and nudges.**
  - READY4K texts: +0.11 SD literacy, +0.15-0.29 SD parent involvement — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20659.pdf)
  - Academic socialization beats homework help — [Harvard GSE](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/09/10/parental-involvement-puzzle)
  - Parent math anxiety plus frequent homework help is linked to lower child math gains — [Maloney et al. 2015](https://sites.temple.edu/cognitionlearning/files/2015/09/Maloney-et-al-2015.pdf)
  - Bedtime Math shared activity improved outcomes — [KQED/NPR](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/52749/how-to-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math)
- **Foundational, short daily practice and diagnostic placement.**
  - About 40% of Grade 7 students in BC have not met numeracy expectations since 2021-22. Experts cite "lack of practice" and weak math-fact automaticity — [The Tyee 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)
  - Early screening is becoming mandatory (BC K, Ontario K-2), and OHRC says Grade 1 screening should focus on decoding and word reading — [BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025ECC0025-000851); [OHRC](https://www3.ohrc.on.ca/en/right-read-inquiry-report/9-early-screening)
- **Non-punitive mastery and usability.**
  - IXL complaints centre on point loss and distress — [Common Sense Media IXL](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/website-reviews/ixl/user-reviews/adult)
  - Khan complaints centre on confusing layout — [Common Sense Media Khan](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/website-reviews/khan-academy/user-reviews/adult)
- **Offline and low-screen activities.**
  - Parents worry about screen volume: 46% (Angus Reid) to ~two-thirds (recent review) — [Angus Reid](https://angusreid.org/screen-time-kids/); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12705222/)
  - Maloney/Beilock suggest puzzles and cooking as everyday math talk — [KQED/NPR](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/52749/how-to-make-sure-your-math-anxiety-doesnt-make-your-kids-hate-math)
- **Bilingual EN/FR (French Immersion).**
  - BC FI demand outstrips teacher supply — [CPF via BCATML](https://bcatml.org/news/canadian-parents-for-french-bc-yukon-release-report)
  - Schools tell parents they don't need French but offer few tools — [Winnipeg SD](https://www.winnipegsd.ca/victoria-albert/french-immersion)
  - Richmond FI parents report lacking program information — [SFU thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2023-09/etd22578.pdf)
- **Indigenous perspectives alignment (First Peoples Principles of Learning).**
  - BC integrates Indigenous knowledge across all subjects' rationale, goals, Big Ideas, learning standards and elaborations — [BC IKP Social Studies K-12](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/curriculum/indigenous-knowledge-and-perspectives/social-studies-K-12-indigenous-knowledge-and-perspectives.pdf)
  - The FPPL include "Learning is embedded in memory, history, and story" and "some knowledge is sacred and only shared with permission." FNESC cautions the principles don't capture any single nation's approach — [FNESC, First Peoples Principles of Learning](https://www.fnesc.ca/first-peoples-principles-of-learning/)
  - The Ministry, BCTF, FNESC and MNBC maintain an Indigenous resource inventory — [BC Indigenous Resources Inventory](https://www.curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/indigenous/indigenous-resources-inventory.pdf)
  - Indigenous student outcomes lag (e.g., the Abbotsford Grade 7 literacy drop), and UBCIC called FSA data essential for tracking First Nations achievement — [The Tyee 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/); [Best Brains Abbotsford (commercial)](https://bestbrains.com/ca-en/abbotsford/article/what-does-bc-fsa-test-actually-measure)
- **Teacher-friendly sharing and alignment.**
  - BC reporting includes student self-assessment of Core Competencies and goal setting — [BC Ministry one-pager](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/pdf/reporting/K-12-student-reporting-one-pager-for-parents-and-caregivers.pdf)
  - Richmond families valued digital portfolios that show learning over time — [SD38 Richmond](https://sd38.bc.ca/node/372)
  - Hill & Tyson note parent methods that differ from teachers' may confuse students — [Harvard GSE](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/09/10/parental-involvement-puzzle)
  - BCTF says FSA adds little beyond teachers' own assessments, so teachers may resist outside "scores" — [The Tyee 2025](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/)

### Inferences
- **Positioning hypothesis (supported in direction, not proven):** "The parent's companion to the BC curriculum." It explains the report card, shows what's being taught this term, runs 10 minutes a day of kind, foundational practice, and gives the parent one script a week. It sits between IXL (paid, punitive, practice-only) and Khan (free, generic, US-sequenced, needs guidance).
- **Report card decoder** is the most defensible *acquisition hook*. It fits search intent ("what does Developing mean"), is province-specific, and is cheap to build. Pairing it with a diagnostic placement turns "Developing in Numeracy" into "these 3 Grade 3 skills."
- **Teacher alignment should mirror school language, not grade the child.** The app should use the same E/D/P/E vocabulary in BC and Levels 1-4 in Ontario, and produce a shareable "evidence of learning" snapshot a parent can bring to a conference. Avoid presenting a competing "score" that teachers or the BCTF may see as conflicting with professional judgement.
- **Indigenous perspectives** should be done with partners (FNESC, local nations, Indigenous educators) and limited to appropriate, permissioned content (e.g., place-based math contexts, authentic texts from FNESC/FNSA lists). A shallow "Indigenous content" badge risks harm and backlash. This is a BC credibility requirement more than a broad acquisition driver.
- **Bilingual EN/FR** is a strong niche differentiator in BC and Ontario FI (neither IXL nor Khan clearly targets FI parents). It is not a reason to enter Quebec, where Alloprof dominates for free.
- **Design quality and kindness are evidence-based differentiators, not cosmetic.** The most voluminous negative reviews of both incumbents are about emotional experience and confusing UX, not content coverage.

### Gaps
- There is no direct evidence (survey or experiment) that Canadian parents would *pay* for a report card decoder or parent coaching layer. Willingness to pay is untested.
- No data on IXL's or Khan's Canadian user base or market share.
- No evidence on whether BC teachers would welcome parent-app sharing. The BCTF's stance on third-party edtech was not researched.
- No Indigenous community perspectives on edtech apps using FPPL were found. This needs direct consultation.
- Effect sizes for parent nudges come from US samples (San Francisco, Chicago). No Canadian replications were found.
